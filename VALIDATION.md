## Evidence limits

These are short controlled Windows/Edge checks. They do not certify full Screen Studio/FocuSee parity, sustained 4K/60 fps, long recordings, crash recovery, all drivers/displays/DPI configurations, native cursor suppression, mobile devices, macOS/Linux, or cloud features. Capture and export currently buffer media in memory; video export is real-time and foreground. WebGL is required for 3D. Cursor/key polling may miss short events.

Camera and microphone recording are intentionally excluded. The sample project is procedural demonstration content, not captured footage from either reference product.

### Audio-gap root cause and fix — 2026-09-14

- The helper previously inserted silence only when a packet arrived more than 20 ms after the preceding packet. The real Media Foundation AAC encoder concatenates PCM across shorter gaps: the synthetic `audio-gap-probe` supplied eight missing 10 ms silent packets before a tone at 1 s, and the encoded tone appeared at **0.9200208 s**, even when decoding with timestamp-preserving resampling. Explicitly filling the gaps restored **1.0000208 s**. This proves a timing-loss bug in the helper/encoder path independently of browser playback or display capture.
- `audio_timeline::gap_frames` now calculates silence for every gap containing at least one 48 kHz sample. The native recording loop uses it before writing the next packet. The half-second late-audio silence-filler policy, overlapping-packet trimming, encoding quality and app offset remain unchanged.
- Regression: `cargo test --release --manifest-path native/studio-capture/Cargo.toml --bin studio-capture` first failed with the old 20 ms threshold (0 vs 480 missing frames), then passed **2/2** after the fix. `node tests/audio-gap.mjs` passed the actual AAC encoder control/corrected comparison (**0.9200208 vs 1.0000208 s**) using only synthetic media. No screen capture or audio playback is used by that test.
- Temporary format logging in `native:check` confirmed 48 kHz stereo PCM was accepted here; fallback-format conversion is not involved in this run. ## A/V drift root cause and fix — office PC — 2026-09-15 evening

- **Dan's hand test, measured offline.** He recorded a YouTube "Audio/Video Sync Test" clip (red circle + beep every second) with Studio Screen 0.4.0 (helper, 3840×2160 at 60 fps) and with NVIDIA's recorder. Beep onsets found by cross-correlation (sub-sample): NVIDIA 14:10 and 14:18 recordings **1.000000 s** apart; Studio Screen takes 14:15 and 14:16 **0.999472 s** and **0.999439 s** apart, residuals ≤ 0.06 ms from a straight line. So the helper lost **~27 samples a second**; audio ran **32–34 ms/min fast** against the picture. Red-circle-to-beep offsets confirm it: NVIDIA flat at 83–84 ms over 8 s; Studio Screen 79 → 75 ms over 7 s. That common ~70–85 ms lag is the clip/Chrome playback on this PC, not the recorder, and explains most of the ~60 ms the helper-only sync test reported this morning (its player is Chromium too).
- **Clock probe (`native/studio-capture/examples/audio-clock-probe.rs`, silent, no capture):** 60 s of real loopback packets while ffplay played digital silence. Device clock vs QPC: **ratio 1.0000000** (48000.00 Hz), so it is not a clock mismatch. Packet stamps jitter ±150–220 ticks (15–22 µs). Replaying the pre-fix `main.rs` arithmetic on those packets: 2,227 trims / 2,283 samples dropped and 705 fills / 1,305 samples inserted (net **−16/s**); a second run net +12/s with 40/s churn each way. The trim rounded up (any early stamp cost a sample) while the fill rounded down (only ≥ 21 µs late added one), so every packet paid a rounding tax in one direction.
- **Fix:** `audio_timeline::align(next, time, frames)` returns Contiguous when a packet's stamp is within `TOLERANCE` (2 ms) of the write position; Trim/Fill/Drop otherwise. `main.rs` uses it in place of the inline arithmetic. The 9/14 gap fix is unchanged: missing 10 ms packets are far outside the tolerance and still fill (`node tests/audio-gap.mjs` still measures 1.0000208 s). No offset, no encoder change.
- **Regression:** Rust `jittered_stamps_lose_no_samples` replays 6,000 packets with ±150-tick jitter and requires every sample written (the old arithmetic loses ~16/s there); `real_gaps_and_overlaps_are_still_corrected` pins Fill(480)/Fill(3840) for missing packets, Trim(240) for a 5 ms resume overlap, Drop, and both tolerance edges. `cargo test --release --locked --bin studio-capture`: 4/4. New `node tests/audio-clock.mjs` (real device, 20 s, silent): 2,000 packets, **960,000 captured = 960,000 written, 0 dropped**, one 773-sample lead-in fill, device ratio 1.0000007; old arithmetic on the same packets: 816 dropped / 1,052 inserted.
- Rebuilt helper: `native/studio-capture/target/release/studio-capture.exe` SHA-256 `DB7F1ACB…61FF`. **Not packaged yet** (0.4.0.exe still carries the old helper); end-to-end confirmation is Dan re-recording the sync clip on the 0.4.1 pack, expected beep spacing 1.000000 s. `a4-av-sync` cannot see drift (16 s, ±10 ms noise) and was not rerun (PC in use).

## Export stall in desktop recordings — 2026-09-15

**Report:** Dan's 0.4.0 hand test (portable built before `5eba2b4`): exports of two helper recordings (4K60, a YouTube A/V sync clip) stall in Premiere and never catch up; the Studio Screen preview and an NVIDIA recording of the same clip are smooth. **Is it in the file?** Yes. The export is 877 frames with pts steps of exactly 1/60 s. Matching every export frame to the raw take (96×54 thumbnails): 88.3% show the raw frame predicted from mediabunny's timestamps (±1, because the clip changes picture every second frame), and only 31.9% show the correct one. The leftover mismatches are spans where the raw picture doesn't change at all (e.g. 3.08–3.53 s and 6.02–6.48 s: 0 changed frames), so any frame there matches.

**Cause:** the helper's fragmented MP4 (Media Foundation) has no `tfdt`. Its moofs hold video traf 1 + audio traf 2, 18 video samples each (0.3 s), and its `mfra` lists video keyframes every 2 s as traf 1 / trun 1 / sample 1, 13, 7, 1, 13, 7… (08.18.21 also has 10, 4, 16). mediabunny 1.56.2 (`isobmff-demuxer.js`) reads the traf, trun and sample numbers and throws them away, and `readFragment` offsets a tfdt-less fragment so that its first sample sits at the tfra time. Rule check: "fragment start = tfra time, then durations" predicts the library timestamp of all 932 + 1,726 + 3,043 + 6,358 frames in Dan's four takes with 0 mismatches. FFprobe reads all four as exactly 1,000/60,000 steps, and a copy of 14.16.48 with only the mfra removed reads perfectly (0 repeats, 0 skips). GitHub main of mediabunny has the same code. History: mediabunny has been 1.56.2 since `4c92370`, the encoder layout hasn't changed since `fb501ec`, and the exporter's MP4 path has read helper takes through `samplesAtTimestamps` since `142402e`, so every build that recorded through the helper has it. `5eba2b4` only changed WebM sources; exporting 6.5–10.5 s of 14.16.48 with the code after it still held a frame at 7.82–8.00 s and ran 11–13 frames behind until 10.07 s.

**Fix:** `patches/mediabunny+1.56.2.patch` (applied by `postinstall: patch-package`): tfra entries keep their traf/trun/sample numbers, each trun records its traf/trun position and first sample index, and a tfdt-less fragment is offset so the named sample (not the first) lands on the entry time. If the named sample doesn't exist, the old behaviour is kept. The helper and `src/exporter.ts` are unchanged. A clean `npm ci` prints `mediabunny@1.56.2 ✔`, and the production bundle contains the change.

**Pin:** `tests/native-export-frames.mjs` (headless Edge). FFmpeg builds an 8 s 640×360 60 fps H.264 + AAC clip (GOP 120, 0.3 s fragments, timescale 60,000) with a 9-bit frame-number barcode. The test hides its tfdt boxes (renamed `free`) and appends an mfra naming keyframes as samples 1, 13, 7, 1. Checks: FFmpeg reads frames 0–479 in order; mediabunny times FFmpeg's original correctly (the control); mediabunny times every fixture frame at n/60 s; three exports (whole at 60 fps, whole at 30 fps, trimmed 1.9–7.4 s) show exactly the source frame for each output frame. **Before the patch:** 252 of 480 fixture frames mistimed; exports wrong on 252/480, 126/240 and 246/330 frames (held up to 12, 12 frames late). **After:** 0 everywhere. An earlier fixture failed on every frame for an unrelated reason (the AAC lead-in stretched FFmpeg's first video frame), fixed with `-video_track_timescale 60000 -avoid_negative_ts disabled` and caught by the control check.

**After the patch on real takes:** `--recording` on Dan's five 9/15 takes: 0 frames timed differently from FFprobe.
